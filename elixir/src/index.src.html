elixir
HOW TO PLAY IT

Elixir by Johnny Hawk is an unofficial browser drum machine, not made or endorsed by Erica Synths. Its sound engine is a JavaScript port of the six drum voices and the FX bus of the Erica Synths LXR-02 firmware (erica-synths/lxr-desktop-app, GPL-3.0, commit a43cd4f), ported line by line; its output matches the original C code compiled for the Cortex-M7 sample for sample. The FX bus (distortion, ring mod, compressor, delay) is the firmware's too; the mixer, the three FX sends, crossfeed and the pattern generator are new. The generator's source kits include Hrtl's free LXR-02 DLC kits, used with credit, and any LXR kit file (.SND) can be opened. Source and licence (GPL-3.0): github.com/johnnyhawk02/johnnyhawk02.github.io/tree/main/elixir.

Crossfeed patches voices into each other: a cell sends the row's sound into the column's voice. Each column picks what it modulates: fm bends the pitch of that voice's main oscillator with the incoming audio (metallic, clangy, a voice into itself gives feedback FM), filter lets the incoming level open (positive) or close (negative) that voice's filter, ring multiplies the two sounds. Open and closed hat are one voice, so the matrix is 6 × 6. Crossfeed is an addition to the firmware's engine; with every cell at 0 the engine runs exactly as verified.

The mixer's level and pan are the voices' own VOLUME and PAN parameters, so they are saved with the kit. Drag a fader for fine control (it moves less than the mouse; hold Shift for finer still) or scroll on it; the strip's name shows the level while it moves.

Save keeps everything as a preset in this browser under a two-word alien name: the sound (kit, crossfeed, FX and sends) and the song (pattern, perform dials, tempo, velocity, accent, master), and loading it brings all of that back. A red star by save means a randomise has changed things since the last save or load. ‹ and › step through the presets; while playing, a new preset comes in at the start of the next bar. Presets opens the preset manager: load, delete, and star favourites; export favourites downloads them as a file, and favourites sent in that way are built into the next release. Open .snd there loads an LXR kit file as the current sound, and export .snd downloads it as an LXR kit file (.SND, without the crossfeed, which the hardware doesn't have).

There are three effects, all the LXR-02's own FX-slot code run as sends: each mixer strip has three send dials left of its fader: echo, dist and ring. Every voice keeps its dry sound and each effect's output is fully wet. The echo's time is synced to the tempo: from 1/128 of a bar fully left to one bar fully right, gliding through the musical lengths (the readout names the nearest, with ≈ when it is between); randomise puts it within 10 ms of a musical length either way (at 120 bpm; the window scales with the tempo). Ticks round the time dial mark the musical lengths (long for straight notes, short for dotted and triplet); a drag catches on them, and the scroll wheel and arrow keys step from one to the next (Shift + arrow for fine steps). The FX section above the mixer sets all three: echo mode (normal or ping-pong), time, tone, feedback and width (100% is the firmware's own; 0% is mono; the firmware's ping-pong only bounces sound that reaches it off-centre, and most drums sit in the middle, so above 100% width pushes the echo's input to one side, and at 200% a centred drum bounces fully; on a normal echo, above 100% widens the echoes' stereo); distortion type (tube, fold or rat), drive, color, tone and volume; ring mod carrier wave and frequency. A kit that used distortion or ring mod in its FX slot loads those settings into that effect with a little of each routed voice sent to it. The sync, the sends, the extra buses and the larger delay buffer are additions to the firmware.

The master section sits after everything: drum 1 sidechain ducks every other voice (before the FX bus) by up to the duck amount whenever drum 1 hits (tick fx to duck the echo, distortion and ring mod returns as well, so their tails pump with drum 1), and the compressor works on the whole mix like the LXR-02's FX-slot compressor; the needle meters show how far each is pulling down, in dB.

The red circle by play opens the recorder: pick free (records until you press stop, up to ten minutes) or a loop of 1 to 16 bars, which starts on the next bar (starting playback from bar 1 if it is stopped) and is cut to exactly that many bars at the tempo, with the echo carried in from before so it loops cleanly. Choose 44.1 or 48 kHz, 16-bit (dithered) or 24-bit, and normalise (peak at −0.3 dB). Each take is listed with a player and a save .wav button; takes are kept until you reload the page.

The sound knobs on the pattern card are performance macros over all six voices at once: fm depth (the drums' FM amount and the cymbal and hat FM), decay (every amp decay), pitch (every voice's coarse tune, up to two octaves either way) and filter (every filter's cutoff). Each sits in the middle as the kit is; turning it pushes every voice's own value towards the top or the bottom, so the kit's differences stay. They change what the engine plays, not the kit: the sound tab still shows the kit's own values, and double-click sets a macro back to the middle. They are saved with presets.

Perform builds the pattern from the dials while it plays: Energy goes from empty through a sparse groove up to the full kit, Beat picks one of sixteen electro grooves (the original five plus eleven after classic records and styles: Clear, Al-Naafiysh, Egypt Egypt, Numbers, freestyle, ghettotech, Drexciya, Detroit, Hip Hop Be Bop, Pack Jam, Sharevari), and Fills sets how often a fill plays (every 8, 4 or 2 bars) and how long and busy it is. The switch on the pattern card turns the pattern off: the sequencer keeps time but plays nothing, and MIDI note-ons from a keyboard or another sequencer play the voices instead (any channel, General MIDI drum notes, velocity sets the level; the card shows the note map and which MIDI inputs it hears; needs a browser with Web MIDI, such as Chrome or Edge).

The steps tab opens the same pattern in the step editor; going back to the perform tab rebuilds it from the dials.

Drag a slider up or down, click along it, scroll on it, or use the arrow keys. Double-click puts it back to the loaded kit's value; a red readout means it differs from the kit.

Randomise (sound tab) varies the selected voice around its current sound, up to the depth, and keeps it sounding like a drum; tick Wild to let every parameter use its full range. In the mixer, r by each name gives that voice a random sound from the bundled kits, varied by 10%, and a varies the voice's current sound by 10%; undo takes either back. The randomise card on the perform tab: kit gives each of the six drum voices a sound straight from a random bundled kit, and alter makes small variations of the current drum sounds (5%, so each stays recognisable); both leave the mixer, FX, crossfeed and pattern alone. Press R to randomise the selected voice; Shift-R randomises every voice at once, up to the depth. Everything builds a fresh sound from voices of the bundled kits (Hrtl's LXR-02 kits and twelve generated ones, which aren't listed as presets), varies each by 10%, and rolls new crossfeed, delay time, echo feedback (usually low, now and then high), beat, energy and fills, keeping the mixer levels; save keeps it.

Swing (on the pattern card, and on the steps tab) delays every second 16th: 50% is straight, 66% a triplet feel, 75% the most. Each track has two dials (under the pattern sliders, and at the end of its row on the steps tab): swing sets how much of the swing that track takes (so the hats can swing while the kick stays straight), and push moves the whole track early (left) or late (right) by up to a quarter of a 16th, centre being on the grid. The groove applies to the perform patterns too and is saved with presets. In the grid, Shift-click toggles an accent (bright step with a bar on top) and Cmd-click (Ctrl-click on Windows) cycles a bar condition: 2 plays the step only on every second bar, 4 only on the fourth. Click a track name to edit that voice on the sound tab. Both hats share one voice, so the open hat chokes the closed one.

HOW THE PORT WAS CHECKED
176 Msamples compared, 6 voices and the FX bus
0samples that differ from the C
700random scenarios: kits, edits, triggers, FX settings, LFO and velocity modulation
−77 dBdifference to the -ffast-math firmware build, init kit

The reference is the firmware's own sound engine (Core/Src/Soundengine: voices, oscillators, filter, envelopes, LFOs, modulation, the parameter handler and the FX bus), cross-compiled for the Cortex-M7 FPU and run under qemu with a small harness. verify.mjs sends the same parameter edits and triggers to both and compares every 64-sample block. The STM32's hardware random number generator is replaced by the same xorshift on both sides.

The port is exact against the C as written. The shipped firmware is built with -O3 -ffast-math, which lets the compiler fuse and reorder float operations, so the hardware rounds slightly differently: with the init kit, the largest difference is 9 LSB. With extreme random settings, self-oscillating filters can drift further apart, because a one-LSB difference grows in the feedback loop.

The engine runs at the LXR's 44 140 Hz and is resampled to your sound card's rate in the browser.
